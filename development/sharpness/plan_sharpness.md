## Methods

User decisions (2026-10-25): version 1 has FFT resolution + astigmatism, decorrelation resolution
and sharpness scores, current slice only. Later: Fourier ring correlation, tile sharpness map over
the field of view (reuses `analyseRegion`), edge spread (line across an edge), bead/particle PSF
fit. No-reference quality scores (`brisque`, `niqe`, `piqe`) were rejected: trained on natural
photographs, they do not measure resolution.

- **FFT cutoff** (Postek and Vladar, NIST SEM sharpness). Power spectrum on a physical frequency
  grid, noise floor = mean power in the spectrum corners outside the Nyquist circle, cutoff = first
  frequency where the smoothed radial SNR falls below the threshold. Directional version in 36
  overlapping 10-degree sectors, centred ellipse fitted to the sector cutoffs -> astigmatism ratio
  and angle. Scan-line suppression drops the fx = 0 and fy = 0 lines of the spectrum.
- **Decorrelation analysis** (Descloux et al. 2019), clean-room implementation from the paper:
  the reference MATLAB code (github.com/Ades91/ImDecorr) has no clear licence, so it was not copied.
  Evaluated with cumulative sums over radius-sorted pixels, about 10x fewer passes than the
  per-radius masks of the original.
- **Sharpness scores**: normalised Tenengrad and Laplacian variance (divided by intensity
  variance), light Gaussian smoothing first.
- **Preprocessing**: periodic-plus-smooth decomposition (Moisan 2011) instead of a window function,
  so the border pixels keep their weight.

Angles are screen angles, counterclockwise from horizontal. The reported astigmatism angle is the
most blurred direction, which is the direction of the streaks in the image; verified visually with
a blur elongated at 30 degrees (streaks at 30, spectrum ellipse short axis at 30, table 30).

## Validation (synthetic data)

Texture: random field with a 1/f^2 power spectrum plus 0.5 % point particles, 512 x 512, unit
pixels, Gaussian blur, white noise as a fraction of the image standard deviation.

| Blur sigma, px (noise 5 %) | 0.5 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FFT resolution, px | 2.19 | 2.87 | 5.18 | 9.45 |
| Decorrelation resolution, px | 2.56 | 3.23 | 5.86 | 11.98 |
| Astigmatism ratio | 1.02 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.01 |

| Noise at sigma 2 | 1 % | 10 % | 30 % | 100 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FFT resolution, px | 4.23 | 5.63 | 6.98 | 10.07 |
| Decorrelation resolution, px | 5.88 | 5.88 | 7.69 | 13.92 |

Decorrelation is the less noise-dependent of the two, as the paper claims; the FFT cutoff follows
the noise level, which is why the user docs ask to compare images of similar dose.

- Elliptical blur, sigma 3 x 1 px, at 0 / 30 / 90 / 135 degrees: ratio 2.50-2.56, worst angle
  recovered exactly (0, 30, 90, 135).
- Horizontal scan-line stripes (30 % per-line offset) on an isotropic image: ratio 2.55 without
  suppression, 1.01 with it.
- 2048 x 2048 region, all three methods: 2.3 s. 512 x 512: about 0.2 s.
- Every second row of an isotropic image analysed with pixel size [1 2]: round spectrum and the
  same resolution in physical units (unit test).

### Pitfalls found while testing

- **`imgaussfilt` truncates its kernel at 2 sigma.** The square, truncated kernel has axis-aligned
  sidelobes near a power of 1e-4 of the peak; at low noise the directional analysis reports them as
  astigmatism (ratio 1.27 for an isotropic sigma 4 blur, 1.02 with a full-size kernel). Even a
  4-sigma kernel (exp(-8) = 3e-4) still showed it. The tests therefore blur in the Fourier domain.
  This says something good about the dynamic range of the method, and nothing about real
  microscope data, whose noise is far above that level.
- **Decorrelation without a peak.** `d(r)` decreases only where the spectrum amplitude falls faster
  than 1/r. A 1/r spectrum running straight into the noise (an undersampled image) gives flat-then-
  rising curves, and so does pure white noise; the two cannot be told apart from `d(r)`. A spectral
  flatness test was tried (mean/rms amplitude: 0.887 for white noise) and rejected: a noisy real
  texture reached 0.864. The method now returns `NaN` ("Decorr.: no peak"), like the original.
  In the same images a flat stretch with a small ripple can pass as a peak at a low radius:
  1/f texture + 30 % noise gave 10.65 px against an FFT value of 3.3 px; cameraman + noise
  11.7 px against 3.75 px. The user docs say to rely on the FFT value when the two disagree.
- The FFT resolution scales with the pixel size only to 1e-4, not exactly: a radius on a bin
  edge can change bins after scaling.

### Noise and SNR

`Noise SD` and `SNR` come from the FFT noise floor (mean power in the spectrum corners, which equals
the white-noise variance with the per-pixel normalisation). Checked against known noise on a 1/f^2
texture plus white Gaussian noise (`noiseProbe`, 2026-10-25):

| Case | True SNR | FFT floor | Autocorrelation (Thong et al.) | Autocorrelation across scan lines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sharp (sigma 1), noise 0.1 | 100 | 101 | -51 (fails) | -51 |
| sharp, noise 0.3 | 11.1 | 11.2 | 17.4 | 17.2 |
| blurred (sigma 3), noise 0.3 | 11.1 | 11.1 | 11.1 | 11.1 |
| any, noise 1.0 | 1.00 | 1.00-1.01 | 1.01-1.06 | 1.00-1.05 |
| noise smoothed along x ([0.25 0.5 0.25]), noise 0.3 | 11.1 | 59-61 | 51 / -136 | 11.3-11.5 |

The autocorrelation method extrapolates the noise-free autocorrelation at lag 0 from lags 1-3 with a
parabola; it fails on sharp images, whose autocorrelation is not smooth at short lags, so it was not
used. Measured across the scan lines only it survives along-scan smoothing, which the FFT floor does
not. The comparison of noise power along and across the scan direction is now done, see "Slow
detector" below. The two-image SNR (cross-correlation of two scans, Frank and Al-Ali 1975) would
come with the planned FRC.

Resolution with correlated noise (`correlatedNoiseCheck`, 2026-10-25; noise smoothed along x with
[0.25 0.5 0.25], 1/f^2 texture, pixels):

| Blur sigma, noise | White: FFT / decorr | Along-x smoothed: FFT / decorr |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5, 0.3 | 5.71 / 6.25 | 2.00 / 2.38 |
| 1.5, 1.0 | 8.94 / 19.6 | 2.00 / NaN |
| 3.0, 0.3 | 9.39 / 10.8 | 2.00 / 10.0 |
| 3.0, 1.0 | 13.0 / 13.5 | 2.00 / 16.6 |

The FFT method collapses to "pixel-limited" because the corner noise floor lies far below the
noise power at mid frequencies; decorrelation is sometimes close, sometimes not. Neither is usable
on such images, which makes the "noise not white" check (noise power along vs across the scan
lines, flag in Note) the most valuable next addition: it would turn a silently wrong 2-pixel result
into a warning.

### Slow detector (real SEM data, 2026-10-25)

Compared with an independent analysis of the same images:
`L:\lsdcc\embi\EM3D\10740_EMBI_Tests_and_Demos\AT\2026\261022_AtlasAT_Test_VL\04_sharpest_analysis\SNR_analysis.md`
(GeminiSEM 460, Volume BSD, 2 nm pixels, dwell 2 us, Variable Liner 8/6/4/2/1 kV; SNR from the
line-to-line autocorrelation, Thong et al.; one-image FRC, Koho et al.; edge width). The tool rows
were on ROI 1 (x 569-1758, y 509-1774), the report used x 496-2142, y 553-1538; the Thong SNR
recomputed on ROI 1 matches the report within 3-6 %, so the region does not explain the differences.

The noise of these images has a pixel-to-pixel correlation of 0.33-0.40 along the scan line (the
report gives 0.4) and none between lines: the detector is a first-order low-pass for this scan speed.
Its response (1-a)^2 / (1 - 2a cos 2 pi f + a^2) with a = 0.4 damps the noise power at the horizontal
Nyquist frequency 5.4x, close to the measured ratio of noise at high vertical frequency to the corner
floor (5.1-5.6 on the slices with weak line jitter).

| VL (slice) | Report SNR | Report FRC 1/7 | Tool before: FFT res. / SNR / astig. | Tool with `SlowDetector`: FFT res. / SNR / noise SD / astig. | Report noise SD | Decorr. | `noiseAnisotropy` / `acrossFlatness` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 kV (1) | 3.43 | 19.0 nm | 4.33 nm / 9.85 / 1.92 at 180 | 22.6 nm / 3.38 / 4910 / 1.23 at 70 | 4811 | 22.2 nm | 10.0 / 0.94 |
| 6 kV (2) | 3.18 | 19.4 | 4.00 / 10.27 / 1.94 at 0 | 24.5 / 2.92 / 5290 / 1.44 at 88 | 5008 | 22.2 | 14.1 / 0.84 |
| 4 kV (3) | 3.38 | 19.6 | 4.42 / 9.85 / 1.95 at 180 | 23.0 / 3.37 / 4920 / 1.38 at 53 | 4794 | 22.2 | 10.0 / 0.96 |
| 2 kV (4) | 3.31 | 19.3 | 4.42 / 9.82 / 1.94 at 180 | 23.8 / 3.25 / 4950 / 1.23 at 70 | 4812 | 20.0 | 10.6 / 0.94 |
| 1 kV (5) | 2.42 | 26.9 | 4.00 / 9.40 / 1.96 at 180 | 30.1 / 2.20 / 5680 / 1.40 at 95 | 5245 | 32.6 | 18.7 / 0.69 |

- Without the correction the FFT branch is useless on these images: pixel-limited resolution
  (1 kV even comes out best), SNR 2.9x too high, and a false astigmatism of about 1.9 along the
  scan direction from the horizontal damping of the noise.
- With the correction the FFT resolution is 12-26 % above the FRC 1/7 value (a stricter threshold:
  SNR 1 per frequency) and ranks 1 kV 33 % worse (FRC: 41 %). Noise SD is 2-8 % above the
  report; the excess grows with the periodic line jitter (0.44 px RMS at 6 kV, 0.94 px at 1 kV),
  which puts power at 0.46-0.47 cycles/line into the noise band. The remaining astigmatism points
  roughly vertical (53-95 degrees), consistent with the report's y-direction loss (FRC y sector
  worse than x), partly also jitter counted as noise across the lines.
- Decorrelation, unaffected by the option, lies 4-21 % above the FRC and ranks 1 kV 47 %
  worse. Slices 1-4 stay on grid values of the first pass (cutoff 0.18 or 0.20 of Nyquist; the
  refinement did not move them), so it resolves only steps of about 2 nm here.
- Sharpness scores: Tenengrad lowest at 1 kV (2.24 vs 2.37), Laplacian variance highest (0.052 vs
  0.048), raised by the noise.
- Not in the tool: per-slice pixel-size calibration (1 kV images are 3.5 % smaller in scale) and the
  line-jitter measurement.
- Small issue seen: `bestResolution` 3.50 nm, below the 4 nm Nyquist period - the ellipse fit
  extrapolates beyond the sector cutoffs. Not changed.

Corrected noise SD equals the SD of the filtered noise (0.3 x 0.655 = 0.196 measured 0.196-0.197).
The flatness test was added after two false alarms of the anisotropy ratio alone, both with signal
up to the vertical Nyquist frequency (`falseAlarmProbe`, not kept):

| Case (white noise) | `noiseAnisotropy` | `acrossFlatness` |
|---|---|---|
| test stack slices 1-5 | 0.95-1.04 | 0.97-1.03 |
| blur 3 x 0.5 px at 0 degrees, noise 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.3 | 138 / 6.9 / 1.15 | 1.86 / 1.71 / 1.09 |
| every second row of a sigma 1 image, pixel [1 2] | 19.9 | 1.40 |

| every slow detector case above, real and synthetic | 4.6-18.7 | 0.69-1.03 |

Rule: `correlatedNoise = noiseAnisotropy > 2 && acrossFlatness < 1.2`.

Alternatives not taken: the line-to-line autocorrelation (Thong) as the noise estimate - gives the
noise SD but no per-frequency floor for the cutoff; a median over the noise rows to reject the line
jitter - jitter is a real degradation of the image and its sidebands fill about a third of the band.

### Plot drawing speed

Redrawing all four plot tabs on every row change took about 0.7 s (users saw about 1 s): uiaxes
`cla(..., 'reset')` re-creates the axes interactions (about 0.1 s per axes) and every `legend` call
costs 0.05-0.17 s; computation is negligible. `updatePlots` now draws only the visible tab and the
others when they are opened: row change 0.15-0.18 s with the Image tab, 0.33 s with the Radial
profile tab (`yyaxis` 0.16 s and `legend` 0.17 s), reopening a drawn tab 0.02 s. Updating persistent
line objects instead of redrawing would save about 0.2 s more; not done.

The Image tab thumbnail first used a 0.5-99.5 % percentile stretch, which looked far harsher than
the MIB view on noisy, low-contrast EM images; it now uses the channel's `viewPort` range and gamma.

## Widget spec for `mib/+views/ImageSharpnessGUI.mlapp`

are written through `BatchOpt.(Tag)`). Leave all callbacks and tooltips empty; `addCallbacks` sets
them. Dropdown `Items` are placeholders, `updateWidgets` overwrites them. Startup function takes
`(app, controller)`. Every component, including grids and panels, is a property of the app (App
Designer does that by default), because `ChildView.getChildren` reads the `Tag` of every child.

Window title `Sharpness and noise`, about 1000 x 680 px. Main grid 2 x 2: column widths
`{300, '1x'}`, row heights `{'1x', 200}`. Settings column (row 1, column 1) holds four stacked
blocks; the plot tab group sits in row 1, column 2; the results table spans row 2.

`ImageSharpnessGUI.m` in this folder is the same layout as App Designer code (typed component
properties, `winController`, `startupFcn(app, winController)`, `createComponents`,
`registerApp`/`runStartupFcn`), copied from the structure of `WhiteBalanceGUI.mlapp`. It stays
outside `mib/+views/` on purpose: on the path it would shadow the .mlapp, and
`Mib3DeploymentReferencesTest` only accepts .mlapp views.

App Designer does not import such a class: R2026a/b open `.m` files only in the plain-text app format
(class derived from `matlab.apps.App`, layout in a companion XML named by `AppConfigFilename`), hence
the error "App property 'AppConfigFilename' not found". `makeMlapp.m` writes the .mlapp instead. An
.mlapp stores the live uifigure in `appdesigner/appModel.mat`; every component carries a dynamic
`DesignTimeProperties` struct (`CodeName`, `GroupId`, `ComponentCode` with `app.ad_CODENAME_ad` /
`app.ad_PARENTCODENAME_ad` templates, `ImageRelativePath`, `DirtyProps`), and the code section holds
`EditableSectionCode`, `Callbacks`, `StartupCallback` and `InputParameters`. The script builds the
components from each `% Create <Name>` block, attaches that data and saves with App Designer's
`MLAPPSerializer`. Once the .mlapp has been opened and saved in App Designer, the .m and the script
are no longer needed.
